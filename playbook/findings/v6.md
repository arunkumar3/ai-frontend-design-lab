# v6 — the page the lab predicted and could not have proposed

Route `/v6`. Commit `d82fbf9`, branch `v6-weekly-radar`. Built after the verdict pass, from
what `RANKING.md` concluded rather than from a lever.

`RANKING.md` §4 ended with a hypothesis:

> A hypothetical `v6` — `v4`'s layout with `v0`'s date grouping — would beat everything
> here, and nothing in this lab's toolkit would have proposed it.

This is that page, plus a palette taken from the artwork. The hypothesis holds, with one
correction to its own terms (§4).

## What this drill is not

**`v6` is not a seventh lever and is not scored against the 17-item tell list.** The other
six drills each isolated one variable against a fixed brief. This one had a human deciding
direction across three rounds of instruction — colour direction chosen from four mockups,
then day grouping, then weekly grouping with an archive picker. Comparing its score to
`v4`'s would be comparing a supervised build to unsupervised ones.

It is recorded here because the *findings* are about the toolkit, and those transfer.

## 1. The prediction was right, and for the stated reason

Nothing automated found the missing axis, before or during this build:

| Instrument | Would it have proposed date/week grouping? |
|---|---|
| `impeccable detect` (live) | No — clean at 0 findings on `v6` and 3 on `v4`, and neither score has any relationship to the axis |
| Screenshot loop | No — it caught seven defects here, none of them structural |
| Community packs | No |
| The constitution | **Only after the fact.** The rule that names it ("any organising axis you remove must be replaced") was written *by* the verdict pass, not before it |

The axis came from reading `releases.js` and asking what the page answers. That remains the
only instrument in this lab that has ever found an absence.

## 2. Page height

1440 light, device pixels at `deviceScaleFactor: 2`:

| v0 | v1 | v2 | v3 | v4 | v5 | v6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6750px | 5574px | 8002px | 7102px | 3742px | 3742px | **3628px** |

**This number is not comparable and should not be quoted as an improvement.** `v0`–`v5`
render all 13 IN titles; `v6` renders one week — 11 of them — with the rest behind a picker.
A page showing fewer titles being shorter is arithmetic, not craft. The honest reading is
that `v6` holds `v4`'s density while adding a header control.

## 3. Live audit: 3 → 0

The three findings `v3`, `v4` and `v5` all carried are gone:

| Finding | How |
|---|---|
| hero eyebrow / pill chip | Eyebrow deleted. It was decoration; the h1 already says it |
| line length > 80 chars | Prose capped in `ch` on the text elements |
| overused font (Instrument Serif / Inter) | Archivo + IBM Plex Mono, neither flagged |

Worth noting how cheap this was. Three findings survived four drills, two of which existed
specifically to raise quality — and all three fell to about twenty minutes of deliberate
attention once someone read what the detector actually said. **The findings were never
hard. They were never prioritised.**

## 4. The correction to the hypothesis: `v0`'s grouping was also wrong

`RANKING.md` proposed restoring `v0`'s **date** grouping. Built as specified, it fails on
the US slate — 9 titles on 9 separate dates, which is nine groups of one, the exact
orphan-row defect `v3` shipped and `v4` fixed. Restoring the old axis literally would have
re-imported an old bug.

Two moves were needed instead:

1. A **timeline rail**, so a group of one reads as a schedule entry rather than a marooned
   card. This made date grouping survivable.
2. Then, on instruction, **weekly grouping** — the publishing unit, since the run is weekly
   on a Thursday. This collapsed IN from 6 groups to 3 and US from 9 to 6, and made the
   rail redundant: with one week on screen it labelled a group of one, so it was removed
   and the week moved into the picker.

**The lesson generalises beyond this repo: "restore what the old version did" is a
hypothesis, not an instruction.** The verdict pass could see that the axis was missing. It
could not see that the axis, as previously implemented, did not fit the data either.

## 5. Defects, and how many were self-inflicted

Seven found across three screenshot rounds plus two audit passes. **Four were caused by
fixes made earlier in the same session** — consistent with `v4`'s finding that one round
ships the regressions it just introduced.

| # | Defect | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline broke over three lines | Original — `ch` cap on a 16px wrapper sized a 56px headline at ~190px |
| 2 | Fallback panels read as holes | Original — tone-c collapsed into the canvas in dark, into white in light |
| 3 | 5-title row broke 4+1, stranding a card | **Self-inflicted** — a card-width bump |
| 4 | 2-title rows flung to opposite edges | **Self-inflicted** — the `1fr` fix for #3 |
| 5 | Cards shrank under their own 150px floor | **Self-inflicted** — a 6-column week at a width that only fits 5 |
| 6 | `opacity` stack failed rendered contrast | Original — caught by the live audit, not by eye |
| 7 | `h1` → `h3`, broken heading outline | **Self-inflicted** — removing the rail removed the page's only `<h2>` |

Defect 7 is the sharpest instance of an existing rule. `CLAUDE.md` already says *after
changing a convention, grep for everything that documents it.* The generalisation this
drill adds: **removing a structural element removes everything it silently carried** — here
a heading level, in the earlier round the entire date axis.

Defect 6 restates `v5`'s finding in a new form. The token pair passed 4.5:1 when computed;
`opacity: 0.75` on top of already-muted text pushed the rendered pixels under. **Computing
the token pair is not the same as computing what ships.**

## 6. One failure of the verification itself

The first data check reported `platform still named on every card` as FAIL. The page was
correct; the assertion looked for `·` separators that are CSS `::after` content and never
appear in `textContent`.

Two minutes from "fix the page" — which would have introduced a real defect to satisfy a
broken test. **When a check fails, confirm the check before changing the page.** This is
the mirror image of the lab's existing trap (a passing test that proves nothing): a failing
test that means nothing.

## 7. What verification is now in the repo

Phase 1 verified by eye and by `impeccable`. `v6` adds three scripts, so the next change
can be checked rather than inspected:

| Command | What it proves |
|---|---|
| `pnpm verify:v6` | 27 render-vs-data checks, both regions — landing week, picker contents and order, archive switching, per-card dates, region-switch fallback |
| `pnpm contrast:v6` | 20 computed contrast pairs, both themes. Tightest 4.53:1 |
| `pnpm palette:v6` | Re-derives the palette from the live poster art |

The palette is the one genuinely new *method* here. Rather than choosing a colour and
defending it, the seven IN posters with artwork were quantised: the slate's dominant hue
band is 10–40° (12,757px of amber/rust/brown) against a 180–210° cyan minority (5,476px).
The canvas is a poster's own brown deepened, the accent sits mid-band, and the cyan is left
to the artwork so the chrome never competes with what it frames. **The provenance is in the
CSS header comment, so the next person can check the claim instead of trusting it.**

Whether this reads better than `v4`'s teal is a judgment. Whether it is *derived* is a
fact, and that is the part worth keeping.
