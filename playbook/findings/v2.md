## The single best decision on the page

**The missing-artwork state stopped being a placeholder and became a design.**

`v0` rendered saturated colour blocks with two-letter initials. `v1` refined those to one
letter and made the collision problem worse. `v2` **abandoned initials entirely and set
the actual title in the display serif** on a quiet warm-neutral panel.

That resolves T5, T6 and T7 in one move, because the whole class of problem — what
abbreviation stands for *108 Base Hospital: Uri* — was invented, not inherited. The data
always had the title. Two drills spent refining a made-up abbreviation; the token system's
discipline ("what typographic role does this element play?") reframed the panel as a
typographic surface rather than an image substitute.

**Generalisable lesson: when a fallback is hard to design, check whether you invented the
thing being fallen back to.**

## Why the colour identity finally resolved

`v1` satisfied "exactly one accent" in the chrome while platform brand colours still
tinted the no-artwork panels — as many as seven distinct colours on the India render, six
on the US render (the `PLATFORMS` map defines eleven; a given region's data only ever
surfaces the subset that appears in it). `v2` confines platform colour to an 8px dot inside
a badge and paints everything else from its own warm cream/amber system.

`v9`'s deepest tell — "no colour decision was ever made" — is now decisively fixed, and
notably it took the *token system*, not the constitution, to do it. The constitution
**stated** the one-accent rule in `v1` and got a partial result. Writing the palette down
as committed values before any component existed got a complete one.

**A rule tells you what not to do. A token system removes the opportunity.**

## The regression

**T4 came back.** Card titles sit below the poster and wrap to one or two lines, so within
the Aug 14 row *Bharat Bhagya Vidhaata* (two lines) and *Aakhri Sawal* (one line) leave
their metadata rows on different baselines. `v1` had solved this. The token system governs
values, not layout invariants — nothing in `tokens.md` can express "metadata rows in a row
must align."

## New tells introduced

**N1 — the no-artwork panel prints the title twice.** Once in serif inside the panel, once
in sans below the card. Six cards carry the same string twice, two inches apart.

**N2 — the featured-card rule is inconsistent.** Aug 12 (*Reacher S4*, one title) gets a
double-width featured treatment. Aug 17 (*Lanterns*) and Aug 21 (*Pyaar Prema Kalyanam*)
are also single-title days and get ordinary cards marooned in empty rows. The rule is
either "single-title days are featured" or it is not; right now it fires once out of three.
This is why T1 is only partial.

**N3 — the page is now 8002px tall**, against `v1`'s 5468 and `v0`'s 6750. Generous spacing
tokens applied uniformly to a list of 13 items produce a lot of scrolling.

## What the implementer said the constraint changed

Worth recording verbatim, because it is a screenshot-loop insight arriving two drills early:

> My first "featured card" attempt used an ad hoc `1.6fr` grid track that, once measured in
> the browser, rendered at the exact same width as regular cards (auto-fill diluted it) —
> the discipline of checking rather than eyeballing forced me to swap it for
> `grid-column: span 2` reusing the existing track template instead of inventing a new
> magic ratio.

The token system's real mechanism here was not aesthetic. It was that **"no arbitrary
values" forces you to justify a number, and justifying it means measuring it.**
