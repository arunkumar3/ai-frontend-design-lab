# v4 — the screenshot loop

Route `/v4`. Started as a pixel-identical copy of `/v3`. The **only** lever: render, look at
all six captures, write down specific defects, fix exactly those. Three rounds.
Commit `7abeb9b`.

The implementer was told nothing about `v3`'s known problems and was blocked from reading
any prior findings. Everything below it found by looking.

## Headline: it works, and it is the biggest single-drill improvement in the lab

**Page height, 1440 light:**

| v0 | v1 | v2 | v3 | v4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6750px | 5574px | 8002px | 7102px | **3668px** |

Half of `v3`. The fix was letting single-title platform groups share a row instead of each
claiming a full-width row of its own — `v3`'s five orphan rows are gone. This was Round 1's
first finding, and it is the tell (T1) that survived every previous drill: unchanged in
`v1`, partial in `v2`, worse in `v3`. **Three drills of design thinking could not fix it.
One look at the rendered page did.**

## The finding that changes how you should use this loop

**Rounds 2 and 3 were spent largely repairing damage done by Round 1 and Round 2.**

16 defects across three rounds. Tracing attribution in the implementer's own critique file:

- **Round 1 — 6 defects.** All genuine pre-existing problems.
- **Round 2 — 5 defects.** D7 and D8 caused by Round 1's flex-wrap fix. D9 caused by
  Round 1's sizing fix ("every poster render at a flat 200px cap"). D10 explicitly labelled
  *"self-inflicted copy regression"* from Round 1's D4 fix. D11 is Round 1's dark-mode fix
  landing short.
- **Round 3 — 5 defects.** D12 caused by Round 2's `basisFor()` change. D13 from Round 2's
  grid fix. D14 from Round 2's D10 fix. D16 exists because Round 2's D7/D8 fix moved two
  fallback cards adjacent.

So roughly **10 of 16 defects were introduced by the loop itself.**

That reads like a criticism. It is the opposite — it is the whole argument for the loop. In
the implementer's own words:

> every fix I made broke something else that I only caught on the *next* round's fresh
> screenshot, never in my own immediate spot-check right after editing

**Practical consequence: one round of screenshot review is worse than useless.** A single
round finds real defects, fixes them, introduces new ones, and ships with the new ones
unseen — leaving the page broken in different places while feeling verified. The value is
not in looking. It is in **looking again after you fix.**

This also explains why the earlier drills' self-verification kept failing. `v1` claimed a
serif font that was not rendering. `v3` shipped a date range its own data contradicted.
Both implementers checked their work — but checked it *before* the change had a rendered
consequence they could see.

## The blind spot — confirmed, and sharper than predicted

Before this drill I recorded a prediction: the loop would catch the orphan rows (a visible
defect) and miss the absent chronological axis (an absence). Absences do not announce
themselves in a screenshot.

**Confirmed. Sixteen defects, and not one mentions date grouping, chronology, or release
order.** A `grep` for chronolog / by date / date group / release order / what drops across
the entire critique file returns nothing.

But the sharper evidence is Round 1's **D4**:

> The H1's "this week" claim is directly contradicted by the date range printed two lines
> below.

It saw the *symptom* of the missing axis — a page claiming to be about a week while
displaying a ten-day span — and **fixed the copy.** It changed the headline to match the
broken structure rather than asking why a page about a week is organised by platform.

**This is the loop's precise limit: it optimises what is on the screen, and cannot see what
should have been on the screen instead.** It will make the wrong information architecture
beautiful, and every fix along the way will be individually correct.

Pair it with a lever that questions structure. On the evidence of this lab, that lever is
references — `v3` got its structural move from JustWatch, not from iteration.

## Round 4 — the design-iterator agent, and a second blind spot

A purpose-built `design-iterator` agent ran one substantive pass after the three manual
rounds (commit `4facea9`). It found something all three had missed, and the reason it
missed them is precise enough to generalise.

**`titlesInBothRegions()` is exported from `lib/regions.js`, has two passing tests in
`releases.test.js`, and was never called by any route.** *Reacher S4* and *Lanterns* each
appear in both catalogs — different platform, different date. So toggling the region
control landed the visitor on the identical poster with no explanation. It reads as a bug
rather than the real cross-market fact it is.

This is the exact detail that made the dataset interesting in the first place — *Lanterns*
on HBO Max Aug 16 in the US and JioHotstar Aug 17 in India — and four rounds of design work
rendered it as an apparent duplicate.

**Why three rounds of screenshot critique could not find it:**

> a screenshot only ever shows one region at a time; the defect lives in the relationship
> between the two toggle states, which only shows up by toggling back and forth or by
> reading the data layer

So the loop has **two** blind spots, and they share a root:

| Blind spot | Example here | Why a frame cannot show it |
|---|---|---|
| **Absence** | no chronological axis | what is missing leaves no pixels |
| **Relationship between states** | cross-region duplicates | a capture is one state |

A screenshot is a single frame of a single state. Anything that only exists *across* frames
— an absence, a transition, a contradiction between two views — is invisible to the method
no matter how many rounds you run.

**And the tests did not help.** `titlesInBothRegions()` had 100% passing coverage while
being entirely unwired. Ten of ten green told us the function was correct and nothing about
whether the product used it. **Dead-but-tested code is the failure mode where a green suite
is actively misleading.**

The fix: cross-listed titles now carry an "Also new in {other region}" line, in neutral ink
rather than the reserved accent.

## The third blind spot — and the only one that was in plain sight

The task review caught what four passes of looking did not: **the footer legend still reads
*'"~" marks approximate artwork'*, but Round 1's D2 fix replaced the `~` marker with an
`(approx.)` label on the cards.** The page explains a symbol that appears nowhere on it.
Every render, every viewport, both themes, both regions.

The first two blind spots had alibis — an absence leaves no pixels, and a cross-state
relationship cannot fit in one frame. **This one has none.** The footer and the cards are in
the same screenshot. Three rounds plus an iterator pass looked straight at it.

Why it survived: critique attention was on card-level layout, because that is where the
previous defect was. **The loop looks where it last looked.** Fixing something creates a
stale reference in whatever pointed at it, and the loop's attention is on the thing it just
changed — not on what described it.

The fixing agent, asked afterwards why four passes missed it, put it better than I can:

> Every review pass fixed and re-checked the *cards*, and verification meant "does the
> card look right," not "does everything that describes the card still match it." Pixel
> judgment can confirm a line is crisp and correctly kerned; it can't confirm the line is
> still **true**.

That is a general rule worth carrying: **after changing a convention, grep for everything
that documents the convention.** A screenshot loop will not do this for you, because the
stale text looks perfectly well-designed. It is legible, correctly styled, properly spaced,
and false.

| Blind spot | Visible in a frame? | Why the loop missed it |
|---|---|---|
| Absence (no date axis) | no | nothing to see |
| Cross-state (region duplicates) | no | one frame, one state |
| **Stale self-reference (footer legend)** | **yes** | **attention followed the last fix** |

## Restraint, recorded

Worth recording that the agent also **declined** a change it considered: reordering platform
groups by recency. It reasoned that doing so would promote a poster-less fallback card into
the page's most prominent slot on the India side — trading one defect for a worse one. It
documented the trade-off instead of taking the improvement. That restraint is itself a
finding: the useful design agent is the one that can say "I considered this and it makes
things worse."
