## What the references bought that iteration could not

**Brand colour finally left the grid.** `v0` let eleven platform colours *be* the palette.
`v1` shrank them to hatch tints. `v2` shrank them to 8px dots. All three kept brand colour
**inside** the poster grid. `v3` took JustWatch's move — stamp the platform once on a group
header — and the grid is now free of borrowed colour entirely. Twenty-two brand marks
became seven.

You do not reach that by refining a badge. Three drills of iteration made the badge
smaller; a reference deleted it. **That is the specific thing reference-driven design buys:
structural moves, not refinements.**

**T4 fixed itself.** Letterboxd applies elevation to the poster image with no wrapping
card. Ragged card heights existed *because there was a card* whose height followed title
wrapping. Removing the box removed the problem — a fix obtained by copying a structure, not
by diagnosing a symptom.

**The footer became honest.** `v3` marks approximate artwork with `~` and explains the
convention in the footer, alongside its sources. It also states plainly: *"Titles without
confirmed art show their name in place of a poster."* It documented its own fallback.

## The failure — and it is the most valuable thing in this lab

**`v3` grouped by platform instead of by date, and that is wrong for this data.**

JustWatch groups by provider because it has hundreds of titles per provider. We have
**13 titles across 7 platforms** — an average of 1.9. The result:

| Platform | Titles |
|---|---|
| JioHotstar | 6 |
| SonyLIV | 2 |
| Prime Video, ZEE5, Lionsgate Play, Sun NXT, Netflix | **1 each** |

Five of seven groups contain a single poster marooned in an empty row. `v0` had three
orphan rows; `v3` has five. **The reference's structure encodes an assumption about its own
data volume, and that assumption did not survive the transfer.**

Worse, it cost the page its primary axis. The product is *"this week's releases."*
Chronology is the reason to visit. `v3` removed date grouping entirely, so answering "what
drops Saturday?" now requires reading small grey metadata on every card in seven sections.

**Generalisable lesson, and the sharpest one this lab has produced: a reference's layout is
a solution to that reference's data shape. Copy the reasoning, not the structure — and
check the structure's assumptions against your own data before adopting it.** The synthesis
said "take the systems, not the pixels," which was not a strong enough warning: structure
is neither system nor pixel, and it is where the trap lives.

## A factual bug the structure hid

The header reads **"13 new titles across 7 platforms · Aug 15–21, 2026."** The India set
includes *Reacher S4* (Aug 12), three Aug 14 titles, and *Pyaar Prema Kalyanam* (Aug 21).
The true span is **Aug 12–21**.

`v0`, `v1`, and `v2` all grouped by date, so a wrong range would have been contradicted by
the group headers immediately below it. `v3` removed the date headers and the error had
nothing to collide with. **Dropping an organising axis also drops the redundancy that was
catching mistakes.**

## New tells

- **N1 — no chronological axis** on a page whose subject is a week.
- **N2 — five single-title groups**, worse than the baseline's three.
- **N3 — stated date range contradicts the data** (Aug 15–21 vs. actual Aug 12–21).

## Carried forward

The missing-artwork panel is now settled and good: the real title, set in italic serif on a
quiet panel, no invented abbreviation, with a footer legend explaining the convention. Three
drills to arrive at "show the title you already have."
